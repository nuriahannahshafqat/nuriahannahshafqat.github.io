Featured project

DJ Set Assistant 🎧

An AI-powered mixing companion for DJs, and my deepest dive into AI-assisted development so far. I got into DJing through a love of music rather than theory knowledge, and working out which tracks blend well took me ages, especially since I love mixing R&B and hip-hop, where harmonic conventions are far less established than in house or electronic. So I built the tool I needed.

Type in the track you're playing and it recommends the best next song from your own library, scored on BPM match, harmonic key compatibility (Camelot wheel), and vibe. The scoring runs in Python with track data from the MusicBrainz API, and the Claude API generates a plain-English explanation of why the transition works, plus alternatives and a preview of the next three tracks.

Designing for Consent Revocation

My final-year HCI dissertation at King's, sparked by something my friends and I know firsthand: someone in a group photo later wishes it were taken down, but asking feels too awkward to actually do. I explored how photo-sharing platforms could let people take back control of photos they appear in, even after initially being fine with the upload, navigating the tension between one person's privacy and the uploader's rights. I designed an interactive Figma prototype of clear, non-confrontational revocation flows and evaluated it through usability testing and interviews with 10 participants.

Key finding: how people want to ask for a photo's removal depends on social closeness: a direct conversation works with close friends, while an indirect route like an anonymous form feels safer with acquaintances.

Camelot Wheel Explorer

An interactive, neon-glow guide to harmonic mixing, the music theory underpinning DJ Assistant. I built it because I couldn't picture what the Camelot wheel actually meant in music theory, so I made a version you can see and hear: click any of the 24 keys to find out which keys mix cleanly and why, with each key's chord synthesized live in the browser from raw oscillators via the Web Audio API, no audio libraries.

Pure SVG and vanilla JavaScript with zero dependencies: fully keyboard-accessible, with a table view, deep-linkable keys, and reduced-motion support.
